    Section 1.7 Doing the same thing again

    Start with A recording an empty square. This program writes the beginning \(0.\text{,}\) then keeps adding the block \(01\text{:}\)
    🔗
    Write(A,0)
Move(A,0,1,0)
Write(A,dot)
Move(A,0,1,0)
Write(A,0)
Move(A,0,1,0)
Write(A,1)
Move(A,0,1,0)
Goto 4
    Here dot is the name of the mark \(.\text{.}\) Lines zero through three are used once. The first trip through lines four through eight leaves \(0.01\text{;}\) the second leaves \(0.0101\text{;}\) the third leaves \(0.010101\text{.}\) Returning to line four returns to an instruction. Card A still records the new square, so the earlier digits remain untouched.
    🔗
    This repeated part of the directions is a loop. It prints \(0.010101\ldots\text{.}\) There is no last digit and no last trip around the loop. There is nevertheless a finite wait for the first digit, a finite wait for the second, and a finite wait for any particular later digit we specify. We can ask for digit one thousand without asking the worker to finish the whole endless decimal.
    🔗
    The loop has separated two things that happened together in our first calculation. The instruction sheet is finished: all its lines are on the page. The answer is unfinished: another trip through the loop adds more digits. The little loop keeps producing its two digits with no arithmetic at all. Long division has a more interesting memory: the remainder left from its last subtraction.
